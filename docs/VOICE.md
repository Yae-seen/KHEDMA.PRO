# Voice — the father mindset

The house voice for khedma.pro. It governs every guide, in French and in Arabic.

## The one line

**Write like a father who wants his kid alive, employed, and not cheated — in that
order.** Not a coach, not a hype account, not a government circular.

## Where this came from, and why not Rachid Niny

The register we want is the one Niny made famous: Darija-inflected, short sentences,
no euphemism, speaks to the ordinary Moroccan without flattering him, and says the
uncomfortable thing out loud. Take all of that.

What we deliberately do **not** take:

- **The name and the byline.** Writing "in the style of" a living, identifiable
  journalist is fine as craft; publishing it on a live site risks readers thinking he
  wrote it or endorses us. We take the register, never the identity or the signature.
- **The political polemic.** Niny's column attacks officials and institutions. Our
  credibility rests on the opposite: we are the boring, accurate people who tell you
  the real deadline and the real fee. A YMYL careers site that picks fights with
  ministries loses the trust that makes its concours pages worth reading — and this is
  a site preparing an AdSense application.

The father mindset keeps the honesty and drops the fight.

## The five moves

1. **Say the hard thing first, plainly.** No throat-clearing. If the odds are bad,
   the first paragraph says the odds are bad.
2. **Short sentences. Then a longer one when it earns it.** Rhythm is how the voice
   stays readable to someone who is tired and on a phone.
3. **Name who will cheat them.** Always concrete: who takes the money, what the fake
   page looks like, what the real address is. A father names the crook.
4. **Never sell a shortcut.** If the honest answer is "this takes two years and you
   need B2 German," say two years and B2. Respect is telling the truth about cost.
5. **End with the next physical step.** Not motivation — an action. Open this page.
   Bring this document. Check this date.

## What the voice never does

- **Never flatters.** No "you've got this," no "your dream is valid."
- **Never scolds.** The reader is not stupid for wanting to leave. He is not a
  cautionary tale. If a guide reads as lecturing, rewrite it.
- **Never moralises about migration.** We do not tell anyone to stay. We tell them
  which doors are real and which are painted on the wall.
- **Never invents an official fact.** Fees, dates, point thresholds and eligibility
  rules are quoted from the official source with a link, or hedged and marked
  unverified. This is the hard YMYL line and the voice does not get to bend it.

## Worked example — the opening we would publish

Target query: `اريد الهجرة الى كندا مجانا` — 1,000 searches/month in Morocco.
"I want to migrate to Canada for free." The most scam-exposed query in the market.

> **Free migration to Canada does not exist. Let's start there.**
>
> Someone told you there is a programme that takes you to Canada for nothing. There
> isn't. Canada charges fees, asks for a language test, and asks you to prove you have
> money in the bank when you arrive. That is not a rumour and it is not our opinion —
> it is written on the government's own site, and we link it below so you read it
> yourself.
>
> Now the part nobody tells you: the word *free* is the bait. The pages promising it
> want a "file fee," a WhatsApp number, a copy of your passport. You will pay, and
> nothing will arrive.
>
> There is a real road. It is slower than the one they are selling you and it is open
> to more people than you think. Here is what it actually costs, what it actually
> asks, and the official page where you check every number for yourself.

Note what that does: leads with the refusal, names the scam mechanism, refuses to
moralise, and hands off to the official source rather than asserting fee amounts we
have not verified. The warmth is in the honesty, not in the adjectives.

## Arabic

The Arabic version is not a translation of the French — it is the same voice written
natively in MSA with Darija cadence. Keep sentences short; MSA drifts long and formal,
which kills the register. If an Arabic paragraph reads like a ministry circular,
rewrite it.

## The test

Read the paragraph aloud and ask: *would I say this, in this tone, to my own son who
is about to do something dangerous?* If it sounds like marketing, it fails. If it
sounds like a lecture, it fails. If it sounds like someone who cares and is not going
to lie to make you feel better, ship it.
